Before anything else

Two things I got wrong.

1. I let one project dominate — you just named it

The site I built makes Voxa the "credibility spine": a full interactive instrument, four paragraphs of narrative, its own nav entry, while five other projects share one collapsed list. That is selling one startup, not selling you. Every direction below is now judged partly on whether it structurally prevents that — not whether I promise to be balanced.

2. I told you WebGL was "table stakes." Against this corpus, that's false.

I based that on Awwwards, where 29 of 47 Sites of the Day used Three.js. But you are not competing against Awwwards winners for a job — you are competing against these 1,910 portfolios. I measured them. In a random sample of 89 live sites, 7.9% load Three.js and 2.2% load GSAP. Real motion and real interaction are not table stakes here. They are almost absent. That inverts the strategy: craft is the differentiator, not the entry fee.

Method

What I actually did, and what I didn't.

  • Parsed the full corpus. Pulled emmabostian/developer-portfolios and machine-read all 1,905 parseable entries — every URL, every role tag.
  • Fetched a random live sample. Seeded random sample of 90; 89 responded. Measured each page's HTML for framework fingerprints, section headings, nav labels, colour tokens and copy patterns.
  • Did not visually rank 1,910 sites. Nobody has. The tiering below is built from the sample, the corpus structure, and the well-known portfolios in it. I am telling you this so you can discount it appropriately.
  • Text counts are lower bounds. 62% of the sample is React and 45% is Next.js; much of their copy renders client-side and never appears in the HTML I read. So when I say 47% have an "About Me" heading, the true number is higher.

Phase 1 · measured patterns

The monoculture, in numbers.

Navigation is effectively one design

Across the sample, the nav labels repeat almost without variation: Projects (29), About (26), Contact (22), Home (21), Experience (17). Roughly four in five sites ship the same five-item bar in the same order. A visitor has navigated your site before they have seen it.

And "AI engineer" is now a crowded label, not a rare one

Of the 1,015 role-tagged entries, 23.4% mention AI, ML or data science. Leading with "AI Engineer" no longer differentiates you — it files you. What differentiates is evidence of taste and system thinking, which is exactly what this brief is trying to buy.

Tiers, by memorability — not by prettiness.

The finding that should drive the whole project

The gap between C and A is craft — spacing, type, restraint, writing. Most people never cross it. The gap between A and S is a single idea worth having. Craft is buyable with effort. The idea is the hard part, and it is what the five directions below are actually competing on.

Phase 2 · principles, not screenshots

What the great sites actually do.

  • One idea, executed to the end. Bruno Simon's lesson is not "a car" — it is that a single committed metaphor can replace navigation entirely. Half-committed metaphors are worse than none.
  • Motion needs one physical model. Active Theory's work reads as a world because everything obeys the same physics. Sites that fail have twenty effects from twenty tutorials. One easing curve, applied everywhere, beats a library of them.
  • Restraint is what reads as expensive. Linear moves almost nothing — but every hover, focus ring and keyboard path is exact. Precision at rest is more premium than motion in flight.
  • Colour as light, not paint. Stripe's gradients read as illumination with a source and falloff, which is why they feel physical rather than decorative.
  • Scroll is a camera. Apple stages content in depth and lets scroll drive a rig. The failure mode is pinning every section — which converts a page into a chore.
  • Constraint becomes identity. Nothing's dot-matrix is one generative rule applied without exception. One rule, no exceptions, is a brand.
  • Interaction must have a referent. The best interactive work manipulates something real — a parameter, a model, a signal. Interaction with nothing behind it is a toy, and readers can tell instantly.
  • The keyboard is a luxury surface. ⌘K, focus states, arrow navigation. Almost no portfolio does this, and it is the single clearest signal of product literacy.

Phase 3 · positioning

Ten roles, one person, no lists.

You asked the site to communicate AI engineer, software engineer, full-stack, creative technologist, frontend, product thinker, system designer, researcher, builder, problem-solver — without ever saying those words. That constraint is correct, and it has exactly one solution: the site must demonstrate each trait structurally rather than claim it.

The balance rule, made structural. The only reliable way to stop one project dominating is to make the homepage a set whose members are equal by construction — an index, a map, a command list — rather than a narrative that has to have a protagonist. Two of the five directions below do this inherently. Note which.

Phase 6 · five directions

Five designers, five portfolios.

Each of these could be built by a different studio. They are not variations.

Direction 01

Signal

The portfolio as a measurement instrument.

Philosophy

Your work is real-time signal processing, so the interface is an instrument panel. Everything is measured, nothing is claimed.

Mood

Oscilloscope at 2am. Precise, warm-on-cold, technical without being cold.

Inspiration

Studio hardware, Teenage Engineering, spectrum analysers, aviation panels.

Typography

Familjen Grotesk display, Newsreader serif prose, JetBrains Mono readouts.

Motion

Envelope easing — fast attack, long settle. One 4.5s breath on live state only.

Navigation

Channel strip. Sections are channels; scroll position is a signal trace.

Homepage

A live scope that will take the visitor's microphone and run real voice-activity detection on them.

3D usage

None. Canvas 2D only — a waveform has no depth to render.

  • The mic demo is genuinely unlike anything in the corpus
  • Authentic — it is literally your day job
  • Cheap to run, 64KB, no WebGL
  • Already built and working
  • Structurally centres one project. The instrument is Voxa's story
  • Reads as "voice engineer" and quietly hides the maths, research and full-stack work
  • Amber-on-black is a strong flavour that may not survive a career pivot

Direction 03 · recommended core

Topology

Your work as one system you can traverse.

Philosophy

You are a systems person. So the homepage is a real graph of everything you have built — projects, techniques, papers, roles — with genuine edges: this used that, this led to that, this failed and became that.

Mood

A control room for one person's mind. Calm, dark, deliberate, quietly alive.

Inspiration

Observable notebooks, dependency graphs, star charts, Obsidian's graph view done with actual restraint.

Typography

A precise grotesque for the map, a serif for the long reads a node opens into.

Motion

Physics. Nodes have mass and settle; the camera eases between focal points; edges tension when pulled. One force simulation drives everything.

Navigation

The map is the navigation. Plus ⌘K and a permanent linear list for anyone who wants to just read.

Homepage

The graph, pre-composed so it reads as a designed diagram at rest — not a hairball. Clicking a node expands it in place into a real case study.

3D usage

2.5D — depth and parallax on the node field, no camera you can get lost in. Canvas/WebGL for the simulation only.

  • Solves the balance problem structurally — every node is peer to every other, and breadth is the visual
  • Demonstrates system design by being a system diagram
  • Almost nothing in the corpus navigates this way
  • Scales: new work is a new node, not a redesign
  • Node graphs are one bad execution away from the neural-net cliché you banned
  • Hard to skim — a recruiter with 8 seconds needs the linear escape hatch
  • SEO and deep-linking need real care
  • The most expensive option to build well

Direction 04

Command

Not a site about a product person. A product.

Philosophy

The strongest proof of product taste is shipping something that feels like a great product. The portfolio is an application: keyboard-first, instant, stateful.

Mood

Linear, Raycast, Vercel dashboard. Fast, dense, unshowy, obsessively detailed.

Inspiration

Command palettes, IDE panes, Superhuman, Arc. Never a macOS clone.

Typography

One tight UI grotesque at small sizes, high information density, exacting optical alignment.

Motion

Interface motion only — 120–180ms, spring-free, functional. Nothing decorative moves.

Navigation

⌘K is the primary navigation. Every project, skill, role and writing piece is a command.

Homepage

An open palette with a live result list. You type your way through his career.

3D usage

None. Depth comes from layering, shadow and focus states.

  • The clearest possible signal of frontend and product literacy
  • Balance by construction — everything is a peer command
  • Genuinely fast; performance is the aesthetic
  • Very few portfolios attempt real keyboard UX
  • Keyboard-first is lost on mobile, where much of your traffic will be
  • Risks feeling cold — hides personality behind an interface
  • Recruiters may not discover the depth without prompting
  • The "portfolio as OS" idea is adjacent to a known gimmick

Direction 05

Field Notes

Explain something so well that the explanation is the portfolio.

Philosophy

The most respected format among serious engineers is the explorable explanation. Each project becomes an essay with a live figure the reader can manipulate.

Mood

Warm, bright, generous, unhurried. A brilliant person showing you how something works.

Inspiration

Distill.pub, Bartosz Ciechanowski, Nicky Case, 3Blue1Brown.

Typography

Book-grade serif for reading, wide margins for marginal figures and side-notes.

Motion

Only inside figures. Motion is always the reader's parameter change, never ambient.

Navigation

A reading list. Notes, not sections.

Homepage

Three or four notes, each with its live figure previewed inline — drag a threshold and see it respond before you have read a word.

3D usage

Only where a concept is genuinely spatial — an embedding space, an attention matrix.

  • The single highest-credibility format with senior engineers and researchers
  • Proves AI depth, teaching ability and research mindset simultaneously
  • <0.5% of the corpus. Effectively uncontested
  • Each note is independently shareable and compounds over years
  • Enormous content cost — one good note is weeks of work
  • You are mid-MSc and working full time; this is the honest blocker
  • Slow to launch; you would ship with two notes and look thin
  • Low visual wow in a 5-second scan

Phase 7 · the argument

Rejecting four of my own ideas.

  • Signal is out as the core. It is the best instrument and the worst fit for your brief. Its entire structure is one project's story. Keeping it would mean overruling the clearest instruction you have given me.
  • The Index is out as the core. It is the most durable and the least persuasive in the first eight seconds. It also proves taste while proving nothing about engineering — for an AI engineer competing on breadth, that is half a portfolio. Its typographic discipline gets absorbed, not discarded.
  • Field Notes is out as the core — on time, not on merit. It scores highest on credibility with exactly the people you want to impress. It also needs months of writing you do not currently have. Shipping it half-done would be worse than not shipping it. It comes back as the project-page format.
  • Command is out as the core, in as the spine. Keyboard-first is the clearest product-taste signal available and costs relatively little — but a command palette is a mechanism, not a concept. Nobody remembers a site for having ⌘K. They remember what the ⌘K opened.
  • Topology survives. It is the only direction that solves the balance problem structurally, demonstrates system design by being a system, and produces a homepage nobody has seen. It is also the riskiest, which is the correct place to spend the risk budget.

The synthesis I actually recommend

Topology as the homepage and the model. Command as the spine. Field Notes as the project format. Index-grade typography throughout.

Concretely: the homepage is a composed map of your work where every node — Voxa, the transformer build, the email clustering, the maths degree, the evaluation research — is a peer. ⌘K and a permanent linear list mean nobody is ever trapped in the map. Opening any node gives a real case study, and the two or three strongest get a live figure the reader can manipulate. The whole thing is set with the restraint of a good book.

That combination hits every requirement in your brief: breadth over dominance, system thinking demonstrated rather than claimed, AI depth made manipulable, product taste in the keyboard layer, and one idea memorable enough to describe a week later.

Phase 5 · brand

The system, if you pick the synthesis.

  • Personality — precise, curious, unhurried, allergic to hype. Speaks in specifics and admits what did not work.
  • Voice — first person, short sentences, no adjectives that cannot be checked. Never "passionate", "innovative", "leveraging", "cutting-edge".
  • Design principles — (1) every element earns its place or is deleted; (2) demonstrate, never claim; (3) one rule applied everywhere beats ten applied once; (4) the reader can always leave the clever thing and just read.
  • Colour — a near-neutral dark ground, one structural blue for connections and machine-reported data, one warm accent for the single thing you should do next. No gradients as decoration.
  • Type — a precise grotesque for interface and map, a genuine reading serif for the long-form. Three sizes of each, never more.
  • Grid & space — 8px base, one measure of 66 characters, and far more negative space than feels comfortable.
  • Motion — one easing curve site-wide; physics only in the map, interface timing everywhere else; a real reduced-motion design rather than a disable switch.
  • Graphic language — nodes, edges and hairlines. No icon set, no illustration, no stock geometry. Diagrams are drawn, never decorated.
  • Cursor — a measurement frame on pointer devices, nothing on touch. It indicates what is actionable; it never trails or glows.

Working name for the direction: Topology. Not necessarily a name on the site — an internal name for the system, the way a studio names a project. If you want a personal brand mark instead, that is question 03.

Phase 4 · what I need from you

Nine questions. Then I design.

01

Which direction — or which combination?

My recommendation is the Topology synthesis. But if the map feels like a risk you do not want, say so and I will build Command + Field Notes instead, which is safer, faster, and still comfortably A-tier. This is the only genuinely blocking question.
02

What are the real edges between your projects?

The map only works if the connections are true. Which project taught you the thing that made the next one possible? What failed and turned into something else? Where did the maths degree actually pay off? I can guess from your repo, but guessing is exactly what would make it a hairball instead of a diagram.
03

Do you want a personal brand mark, or just your name?

A monogram and a wordmark are cheap to make and they change how the site is remembered. Do you have anything existing — a signature, initials, a logo you have used?
04

Photography.

One portrait against a plain wall in soft window light, and one candid working shot. The current headshot is formal studio grey and fights every direction above. Also: are you comfortable being on the site prominently, or would you rather the work carried it?
05

What can you publish about Voxa?

Still outstanding from last round, and still the highest-value item. Real latency numbers, real volumes, what is under NDA. I will not invent metrics and attribute them to you.
06

Do you write, and would you?

The Field Notes layer needs two or three pieces of real writing. If you have drafts, notes, or even good LinkedIn posts, send them. If you would rather not write, tell me now and I will design a version that does not depend on it.
07

Who is the primary audience?

Indian AI startups, international labs, freelance clients, or graduate admissions? Each shifts how much the site leads with depth versus breadth. If it is "a serious AI company", that changes the map's centre of gravity.
08

Websites and designers you personally admire.

You gave me a long inspiration list, but not your taste. Three sites you like and one you hate would tell me more than the whole list.
09

Timeline, and what happens to the current build.

The Signal site is complete and working on branch amir3-rebuild. Options: ship it now as an interim and build Topology behind it; keep it purely as a reference; or delete it. It cost a session — do not keep it out of sentiment.
